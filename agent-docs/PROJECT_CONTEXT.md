# Code-MoR Project Context

Last updated: 2026-08-29

## 1. Project purpose

Code-MoR is PayTech's lightweight Merchant of Record platform blueprint. The platform lets a Client send a buyer to a hosted checkout, processes the transaction through MIDs held by an Operating Entity, records all financial participants and costs, calculates Client and Local Partner entitlements, and supports settlement and post-transaction operations.

This repository currently contains the interactive requirements and architecture documents plus an initial local-development foundation. It is not yet the production Java implementation.

## 4. Participants

- **Client**: platform customer. A Client may be an `INDIVIDUAL` or `ORGANIZATION`. Client is the canonical name; do not rename it Merchant.
- **Operating Entity**: legal seller that enters provider/acquirer agreements, obtains MIDs, and is represented in the ledger. Its source is exactly one of `PLATFORM`, `CLIENT`, or `PARTNER`: a platform-owned entity, an Organization Client's own Party or a separately reviewed associated Party, or an entity supplied by a Local Partner. Every Operating Entity is managed by the platform after onboarding.
- **Local Partner**: external collaborator that submits its legal-entity information, receives a separately configured share, views its entities/MIDs/transactions, and requests payout. Its transaction access is read-only.
- **Consumer**: final buyer who pays through Hosted Checkout.
- **Provider / Acquirer**: external payment channel such as NI or Geidea. `mada` is a card scheme/payment network rather than the acquiring company itself.

A Local Partner and an Operating Entity are distinct roles: the partner is the commercial relationship and console user; the entity is the legal seller and MID holder. One Local Partner may supply multiple entities, and one Organization Client may apply with its own Party or a separately reviewed associated Organization Party. CCT records only `SAME_PARTY` or `VERIFIED_ASSOCIATION` as the Client-source basis; it does not currently model multi-company or multi-level ownership paths or calculate indirect ownership. Neither source role may select an Operating Entity or MID in a transaction request. CCT resolves the Operating Entity before Commerce opens the Order; Payment selects a MID under that frozen entity for each Attempt. Temporary Provider/MID shortage leaves the Order and Intent available for a later Attempt under the same entity. `OperatingEntityChanged` with `operating_entity_status=CLOSED` permanently blocks new Attempts; after active and `UNKNOWN` Attempts have ended, Payment terminates the Intent and Commerce cancels the open Order/Session. There is no automatic replacement or cross-entity retry; a new Client-created Order requires a new `client_order_id`.

Cancel is Commerce-owned and applies only before any authoritative `AUTHORIZE`, `CAPTURE` or `SALE` success; submitted or `UNKNOWN` Provider Actions must first resolve, and Commerce closes Order/Session only after definitive Payment Intent termination. Void is Payment-internal for a successful uncaptured Authorization (`captured_amount=0`) and creates no Commerce Refund Request, Finance cash reservation/Journal, Fiscal Fact or Credit Note. Reversal is a Commerce-owned orchestration command: Payment classifies atomically under lock, routes an uncaptured Authorization to Void, and routes captured or `SALE` value through the existing full Refund chain. `REVERSAL` is not a Payment Action type, and direct public Void is not exposed.

Refund authorization is deliberately not a Finance concern. Commerce reserves its Order Refund Position and directly calls PAY-I04; Payment reserves its own Refund Position and executes the Channel Refund. `UNKNOWN` remains local to Payment, `FAILED/CANCELLED` notify only Commerce, and `SUCCEEDED` notifies Commerce and Finance. Finance only posts the successful refund, first reducing Client PENDING then AVAILABLE; LOCKED and the prepaid Security Deposit are not consumed by `RefundSucceeded`; any amount not covered by PENDING then AVAILABLE becomes Client Recovery Receivable. Unresolved refunds do not block contractual balance maturity.

## 5. MVP product scope

- Begin with one UAE Operating Entity while preserving a model that can support multiple countries and entities.
- Hosted, redirect-based Checkout owned by the platform.
- USD and one-time payments first; monetary records must still carry currency explicitly.
- Multiple providers, acquirers, MIDs, routing rules, and failover paths are expected.
- A pre-created Offering is not required. The Client supplies product/service details at order creation. Commerce stores monetary and classification facts on the Order and exactly one immutable Order Context for transaction-time references.
- The Order and Order Context together preserve the seller identity and contractual, tax, legal and refund facts used for the transaction without a Commerce aggregate Schema/Hash.
- Consumer Context stores a Client-namespaced non-PII opaque `consumer_reference`, stable Email/optional Phone Vault Tokens, a separate Profile Token containing only name and full billing address, and approved derived dimensions. It stores no Consumer Blind Index, Key Version or Masked Value. Immutable Checkout Network Evidence directly stores `client_ip_address`, `user_agent`, `trusted_proxy_chain` and approved dimensions; it has no Vault Token, Blind Index/Key Version or device identifier.
- Payment Acceptance Readiness is not a stored summary. COM-I10 validates the three required immutable Acceptance records and their same-Order/Session `LEGAL_ACCEPTANCE` evidence on every call; Payment freezes the three explicit IDs and completion time.
- Each Order freezes exactly one accurate `industry_code + mcc_code` pair from the same CCT-I13 Profile/Classification Proof and `commercial_context_hash`; Payment, Risk and Finance inherit that pair and do not reread a current Profile to alter historical facts.
- Full and partial refunds may be initiated by the Client or internal operations.
- Cancel closes only an unpaid Order, Void releases only an uncaptured successful Authorization inside Payment, and Operations Reversal delegates the atomic Void-versus-full-Refund decision to Payment.
- Manual Payout amounts and fees are calculated by the system; payout initially uses fixed dual approval, a pre-created Execution, manual bank submission, payment evidence, and complete `CONFIRMED` debit reconciliation before `PAID`. Provider Settlement is a separate reconciliation concept.
- Client pricing supports percentage plus fixed fees.
- Each Local Partner has an independently configured revenue-share rule.
- Industry eligibility is a configurable allowlist/rule set, never a hard-coded game-industry branch.

## 6. Core systems

Exactly six systems own the core business capabilities:

1. **Client & Contract** — Party and individual/company profiles, Client/Local Partner/Operating Entity roles, contracts, consumer legal terms, commercial pricing, entity assignment, relationship facts and point-of-use transaction-eligibility validation. It does not persist an Eligibility aggregate. Risk owns KYC/KYB decisions and Finance owns Beneficiary Accounts and Manual Payouts; this system does not own orders, payments or ledger entries.
2. **Commerce** — order creation, one immutable Order Context per Order, current Consumer Context, immutable Checkout Network Evidence and Consumer Contract/Acceptance facts, consumer-facing commercial context, order lifecycle, and refund intent. It does not route payments or calculate accounting entries.
3. **Payment Orchestration** — payment intents/attempts, provider adapters, MID selection, routing, provider callbacks, refunds, and payment/dispute event normalization. It does not own Client contracts or the financial ledger.
4. **Risk & Compliance** — transaction risk decisions, rule configuration, KYC/KYB support decisions, sanctions/compliance checks, cases, and review outcomes. It does not execute payments or settlements.
5. **Finance** — pricing and partner-share calculation, multi-party double-entry ledger, balances, Provider settlement/remittance reconciliation, Client/Local Partner Manual Payout, payment evidence, prepaid security-deposit Accounts, and Journal-based post-Payout recovery. It does not alter payment-provider transaction state.
6. **Platform Support** — external-IdP identity binding and authorization, API credentials, business Email OTP, files/evidence, consumer documents, Email/notification delivery, Client Webhooks, security/audit trails, Gateway protection-policy publication, technical configuration and its own reliable technical execution. MVP generic Retention/Legal Hold execution is deferred; Platform does not publish retention events or run cross-system destruction. Business idempotency, local `compensation_event` records and domain histories remain local to each owning system. Platform Support does not orchestrate business flows, centrally execute other systems' compensation tasks or own financial facts.

### 6.1 Supporting technical infrastructure

The following components support the six systems without becoming additional core business systems:

- **Vault Service** is a generic sensitive-value and cryptographic service. It protects explicitly classified recoverable non-card sensitive values, creates no Consumer/Customer/Order/Network Evidence business table, and never owns business meaning. Commerce Email and Phone use the approved stable Value Tokenization mode: equal normalized values for the same field return the same opaque Token, encryption/key rotation does not change that Token, and destruction waits until no authorized references remain and Retention/Legal Hold permits it. The separate Consumer Profile Token contains only name and full billing address. Stable Tokens may cross Commerce→Payment→Risk only in an internal synchronous minimum contract and never in external APIs, ordinary logs, MQ or BCR. Network Evidence does not enter Vault. PAN, CVV/CVC, full track data and PIN/PIN blocks are excluded.
- **`compensation-event` library** is embedded in each service and persists work only in that service's database. Business changes and events commit atomically; execution begins after commit and XXL-Job provides fallback scanning or sharding. Database Claim/Lease protects multi-instance execution. Successful rows may be deleted; failed rows remain visible for alerting and manual reopening. Consumers implement idempotency in their own business facts and projections rather than a generic Inbox.

Vault is internal-only, uses mTLS/workload identity and purpose-bound authorization, and is not exposed through Gateway or a BFF. The Code-PMX Vault and compensation repositories are implementation references; their current code is not automatically production-approved for Code-MoR. Detailed rules are in `VAULT_SERVICE_ARCHITECTURE.md` and `COMPENSATION_EVENT_GUIDE.md`.

The Java and middleware baseline is confirmed in `TECHNOLOGY_STACK.md`: Java 25 LTS, Maven, Spring Boot 4.0.8, Spring Cloud 2025.1.3, Spring Cloud Alibaba 2025.1.0.0, Nacos 3.1.x, Spring MVC for core services/BFF/Vault, Spring Cloud Gateway WebFlux, MyBatis, MySQL 8.4 LTS, Flyway after database implementation approval, RabbitMQ, Redis/Lettuce/Redisson, XXL-Job and the OpenTelemetry/Prometheus/Loki/Tempo/Grafana stack. The Maven Wrapper, unified BOM/Parent, common-core, Snowflake ID and MVC web-starter modules are initialized and their `1.0.0-SNAPSHOT` artifacts are published to the existing local Nexus. `LOCAL_DEVELOPMENT_ENVIRONMENT.md` defines the hybrid Mac topology; its `base` Compose Profile has passed a local smoke test and is currently stopped with the three persistent volumes retained.

## 7. Entry applications

Entry applications expose core-system capabilities but do not own core business facts:

- **API Gateway**: common ingress, authentication enforcement, rate limiting, routing, request IDs, and access logs.
- **Hosted Checkout**: Consumer payment experience.
- **Client Console**: onboarding, transaction history, statements, refund requests, and payout requests.
- **Partner Console**: entity submission, MID/entity/transaction queries, revenue-share statements, and payout requests. Order and payment operations are forbidden.
- **Client API**: programmatic order, checkout, transaction, refund, and statement access.
- **Operations Console**: internal review, configuration, payment/refund support, reconciliation, settlement, compliance, and audit workflows.
- **Provider Callback Endpoint**: receives and authenticates provider webhooks before Payment Orchestration normalizes them.
- **Consumer Service Portal**: receipt/invoice access and permitted post-purchase self-service.

Entry applications are logical channels, not one-to-one Java services. The current deployment baseline consolidates Hosted Checkout and Consumer Service Portal into `checkout-web`, consolidates Client and Partner consoles into `business-portal-web`, and introduces one stateless `business-portal-bff` for those external consoles. Client API is routed to owning core systems and Provider Callback is implemented by Payment Orchestration. The separately secured Operations frontend/BFF is an MVP logical requirement for refund, dispute, reconciliation and settlement operations, but has not been scaffolded. See `APPLICATION_ARCHITECTURE.md` for the authoritative mapping and boundaries.

## 8. Cross-system interaction rules

- Every core system owns an independent database; no service may read or write another system's database.
- Synchronous commands and immediate queries use HTTP. Internal Java consumers depend on the owning service's lightweight `*-api` contract JAR and call its `@HttpExchange` interfaces through Spring HTTP Service Clients; Nacos and Spring Cloud LoadBalancer provide discovery and instance selection.
- MQ carries internal callbacks and business facts. Consumers must be idempotent and tolerate duplicate or out-of-order delivery.
- A producer writes its business change, history and `compensation_event` in one local transaction. It publishes only after commit; XXL-Job is a recovery wake-up and database Claim/Lease remains the final multi-instance exclusion boundary.
- The MVP has no generic Inbox or Outbox. Consumers persist `source_event_key`, payload hash and source version in the receiving business fact or projection and acknowledge only after the local transaction commits. Current or otherwise retained keys reject duplicate-key/different-payload conflicts and version rollback; domains that must detect conflicts for every historical event retain each Key/Hash in an immutable business History/Fact rather than claiming a current-only projection provides that guarantee.
- Recoverable non-card sensitive values never cross system boundaries as plaintext or locally copied ciphertext. The default contract returns an owner-controlled derived fact; a Masked Value is added only for a demonstrated ordinary display consumer. By default, a long-lived recipient Token is allowed only when Vault `CopyForBinding` creates a recipient-specific Record/Token and Binding. The sole approved V1 exception is the stable Consumer Email/Phone Value Token carried through the internal synchronous Commerce→Payment→Risk minimum contract: Payment only passes it through, Risk uses it under an approved Assessment Purpose, and it never enters MQ, external APIs, ordinary logs or BCR. Holding any Token does not grant Reveal; each service may reveal only the minimum field for an authorized purpose and must emit a sensitive-access audit fact.
- PAN, CVV/CVC, full track data and PIN/PIN blocks must not enter Code-MoR services, databases, events, logs or Vault. Payment retains only provider token references and approved minimal card metadata such as scheme, BIN, last four digits, issuer country and provider fingerprint; that metadata still requires explicit classification, masking and retention.
- Cross-system consistency is eventual unless an explicit synchronous validation is required before accepting a command.
- Entry applications and Gateway contain no source-of-truth business state.
- Finance entries are append-only/immutable; corrections use compensating entries rather than mutation.
- External provider callbacks are authenticated, deduplicated, normalized, and then published internally.

## 10. Money lifecycle

The primary entitlement lifecycle is:

`Client/Partner Pending -> Available -> Locked for Payout -> Paid（成熟时间由冻结的 Client Agreement-OE 规则决定）`

Post-Payout refunds or disputes may create a customer-facing negative position, but the accounting fact is an asset receivable:

`Risk Minimum Deposit Requirement -> Prepaid Security Deposit Account -> Four Manual Payout Checkpoints`; for `DisputeLost/DisputeOutcomeCorrected`, only `outcome=LOST + principal_bearer=CLIENT + security_deposit_use_allowed=1` may consume the deposit, then any shortfall becomes `Client Recovery Receivable -> Future Netting or Manual Receipt`

Finance is responsible for calculating and posting distinct amounts for at least the Consumer payment, provider/acquirer costs, platform revenue, Client entitlement, Local Partner share, taxes where applicable, prepaid security-deposit movements, refunds, disputes, Provider settlement movements, and Manual Payout movements. Calculation rules are versioned business inputs; the ledger records the resulting immutable facts.

For a Partner-sourced Operating Entity, the transaction OE is not assumed to be the platform profit entity. CCT owns the Partner Agreement, its unique Platform Signatory Party and the explicit Platform Service Charge term; Commerce freezes the selected Scope and Party in the immutable Order Context. Finance maps that Party to one manually maintained ACTIVE Finance Entity, then posts two separately balanced Journals: expense/payable in the Partner OE Book and receivable/revenue in the Platform Finance Entity Book. The charge is contract-derived, never a residual, and each Book performs its own Trial, Close and profit carry-forward.

## 11. Naming and modeling decisions

- Keep **Client** as the canonical platform-customer term, with individual/company represented by type rather than two competing role names.
- Keep **Local Partner** separate from **Operating Entity**.
- Treat Hosted Checkout, consoles, public APIs, callbacks, and Gateway as entry applications, not additional core systems.
- Treat Vault as a security infrastructure service rather than a seventh core business system; it protects values but does not own their business meaning or lifecycle decision.
- Use the per-service `compensation_event` pattern for MVP reliable asynchronous work; do not introduce generic Inbox/Outbox or a central compensation database without a new architecture decision.
- Do not require an Offering aggregate for MVP; preserve commercial details through the Order, its Items/Legal records and the immutable Order Context.
- Do not infer industry-specific behavior in code. Use configured capabilities and eligibility rules.
